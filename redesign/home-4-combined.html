Every P2P exchange today has the same architecture.

The cryptocurrency is decentralized. The marketplace is not. Behind almost every peer-to-peer exchange:

  • A single company owns the servers
  • A single company controls the database
  • A single company decides which advertisements appear
  • A single company resolves disputes
  • A single company stores reputation scores
  • A single company may suspend or remove users
  • A single company decides which countries are supported
  • A single company can be shut down, hacked, or pressured

If the operator fails — technically, financially, legally — the marketplace disappears, even though the blockchain underneath never stopped. OpenFiat eliminates the dependency.

Two layers, each doing what it does best.

The architecture minimizes on-chain cost while preserving decentralized ownership and transparency.

One trade, four moves

Protected from the second move onward — nobody in the middle can take the funds, and nobody has to trust the other side's goodwill.

01 · NETWORK

Reserve

You reserve part of a merchant's advertisement; every node records it.

02 · ON-CHAIN

Escrow locks

The seller's stablecoin moves into an escrow vault keyed by your reservation. Check it exists before you pay.

03 · FIAT RAIL

You pay

Bank transfer, mobile money or cash, directly to the merchant — the protocol never touches fiat.

04 · ON-CHAIN

Escrow releases

The merchant confirms and the program pays you. Disputes go to staked arbitrators.
